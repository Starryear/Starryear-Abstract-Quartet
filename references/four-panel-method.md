# Four-panel method

Use these directions as one continuous art-direction system, not four independent prompts. The goal is not variety for its own sake. It is a controlled transformation of one photographic instant:

`reality → memory → abstraction → reinterpretation`

## 1. Analyze and lock the source

Before generating, write a compact internal source map. Do not invent answers that are not visible in the photograph.

- Scene identity: What makes this place or moment unmistakably itself?
- Spatial layers: What belongs to foreground, middle ground, and background?
- Dominant geometry: Which horizon, roofline, path, wall, shoreline, branch, figure, or shadow organizes the frame?
- Light: What is its direction, softness, contrast, color temperature, and brightest opening?
- Visual weight: Where are the dense and quiet areas? Where does the eye enter and settle?
- Rhythm: Which repeated windows, trunks, ripples, steps, reflections, posts, or silhouettes create cadence?
- Material evidence: Which stone, water, glass, wood, foliage, mist, paper-like surface, or atmospheric grain matters?
- Emotional temperature: Is the moment cool, warm, still, distant, fragile, humid, solitary, suspended, or quietly alive?
- Negative space: Which empty area gives the scene breath?

Choose three to five **scene anchors** that must survive across Panels 2–4. They should be source-specific, visually legible elements rather than generic style traits. Also choose one **emotional invariant** and one **light invariant**.

## 2. Establish one framing system

Derive a single 2:1 landscape crop from the original photograph. Use the smallest crop that produces a convincing composition. Protect the main subject, essential spatial relationships, and meaningful negative space.

Use this same frame as the coordinate system for every panel. Major anchors should remain in approximately corresponding positions. Do not independently recrop each panel.

If the original aspect ratio cannot reach 2:1 without destroying the scene, extend only inconspicuous edge atmosphere when technically necessary. Never stretch the photograph.

## 3. Generate separately, assemble afterward

Create Panels 2, 3, and 4 separately at the same 2:1 dimensions. Use the source photo as reference for each panel. Do not generate the entire quartet in one pass: that often alters Panel 1, adds separators or typography, and breaks spatial continuity.

After the three interpretations are approved, place the actual cropped photograph at the top and assemble the four panels with a deterministic compositor. Do not ask an image model to recreate the original photograph.

## Panel 1 — Original photograph

### Function

Panel 1 is evidence and the visual contract for everything below it.

### Direction

Use the original photographic material unchanged apart from the approved 2:1 crop, color-profile conversion, and technical resizing needed for composition. Preserve pixels, color, texture, light, exposure, grain, and subject identity.

### Never do

- Never regenerate, redraw, beautify, retouch, relight, recolor, denoise, sharpen excessively, remove objects, or add objects.
- Never apply a paper texture, illustration style, cinematic grade, or AI “enhancement.”
- Never add labels or captions.

## Panel 2 — Starryear Memory Translation

### Function

Panel 2 is the scene remembered emotionally. It should still carry photographic residue, but details begin to soften, merge, or drift.

### Direction

Transform the photograph into an editorial visual memory piece.

Keep the original spatial structure and emotional atmosphere. Do not simply apply filters. Extract the essential light, texture, composition, silhouette, rhythm, and negative space.

Create a poetic editorial abstraction with soft paper texture, subtle material feeling, minimal artistic composition, and a museum-catalog sensibility. The image should feel like a remembered scene, not an illustration. Preserve the photographic origin and avoid excessive fantasy.

Let the largest source shapes remain readable. Allow secondary details to dissolve into quiet tonal fields, faint transfers, soft-edged fragments, restrained grain, or translucent material layers. Keep one or two photographic traces sharper than the rest so the memory still has evidence.

Use a restrained palette derived from the photograph. Paper texture should be fine and tactile, never grungy, vintage-themed, or decorative. Preserve breathing room.

### Avoid

- one-click blur, duotone, double exposure, or generic film filter;
- scrapbook collage, torn-paper gimmicks, stickers, tape, handwriting, frames, or printed captions;
- painterly brushwork, fantasy fog, surreal objects, or unrelated symbolism;
- flattening the whole scene into the same softness.

## Panel 3 — Starryear Structural Distillation

### Function

Panel 3 is the scene understood structurally. It reduces detail more strongly than Panel 2 but must remain traceable to the same real place.

### Direction

Abstract the photograph into a minimal visual study.

Analyze foreground, middle ground, background, light direction, main shapes, and visual balance. Reduce unnecessary details, then reconstruct the scene with minimal composition, clean geometry, quiet atmosphere, Japanese contemporary design sensibility, and visual-research discipline.

The result should look like an analytical abstraction of a real place, not a painting. Keep the original emotional temperature.

Translate spatial layers into a small number of proportionally accurate masses, planes, lines, gaps, and tonal fields. Preserve the hierarchy of the scene: the dominant mass remains dominant, the main opening remains open, and the light still travels in the original direction.

Use clean but not sterile geometry. Permit slight organic irregularity when the source contains water, foliage, mist, stone, or human presence. Use negative space as structure rather than empty decoration.

Derive colors from the photograph and reduce them to a controlled system. One accent may be retained only if it already exists in the source or expresses its light.

### Avoid

- a miniature vector redraw containing every object;
- arbitrary circles, grids, Bauhaus shapes, red suns, Japanese characters, or fashionable symbols not evidenced by the source;
- generic architectural-diagram styling;
- high-chroma color unrelated to the original scene;
- painterly, watercolor, anime, or concept-art rendering.

## Panel 4 — Hybrid fusion

### Function

Panel 4 is not a compromise between Panels 2 and 3. It is a new conclusion: the structure is understood, then remembered again.

### Direction

Combine the emotional memory language of editorial abstraction with the structural discipline of travel-photo analysis.

Aim for half documentary photography and half abstract memory. Keep the original scene identity, light, and spatial relationships. Add poetic atmosphere, minimal structure, quiet Japanese sensibility, and a sense of time and distance.

The result should feel like a real place remembered through a dream. It must be a synthesis rather than a simple blend or crossfade of Panels 2 and 3.

Retain at least one unmistakably photographic fragment or material trace, one clearly distilled structural relationship, and the original light invariant. Let these elements interact instead of sitting in separate halves.

Panel 4 may be the most atmospheric panel, but it must not become the least recognizable. Its abstraction should feel earned by the previous stages. Keep the same emotional temperature while increasing the sense of time, distance, and afterimage.

### Avoid

- a 50/50 split screen;
- a basic opacity blend of Panels 2 and 3;
- a dramatic fantasy climax, surreal portal, glowing figure, or invented landscape;
- excessive haze that erases all scene anchors;
- treating “dream” as permission for unrelated imagery.

## 4. Continuity rules

The viewer should recognize one uninterrupted memory moving downward. Enforce these rules across Panels 2–4:

- Keep the approved crop and broad spatial proportions.
- Preserve the scene anchors, though their level of detail may change.
- Preserve the light invariant: direction, brightest zone, and broad contrast relationship.
- Preserve the emotional invariant. Do not turn a quiet cool scene into a warm dramatic spectacle.
- Keep the source-derived palette family. Reduce or mute colors before inventing new ones.
- Let abstraction increase from Panel 1 to Panel 3; let Panel 4 restore selected photographic evidence without returning to realism.
- Vary treatment, not identity. The panels should be related but not repetitive.

## 5. Composition and assembly

- Canvas ratio: exactly 1:2 portrait.
- Four equal 2:1 landscape panels stacked vertically.
- Recommended master size: 1600 × 3200 px; each panel 1600 × 800 px. Other sizes are allowed if the ratios remain exact.
- Panel order: original, editorial memory, structural abstraction, hybrid fusion.
- No borders, gaps, gutters, frames, margins, dividers, or background strips.
- No labels, text, numbers, logos, signatures, captions, or watermarks inside the artwork.
- Align all panel edges exactly. Avoid accidental one-pixel seams.
- Use consistent color management and export as a high-quality PNG or JPEG.

## 6. Quality-control gate

Check the completed artwork at full size and as a small thumbnail.

### Source fidelity

- Panel 1 is the actual source crop, not an AI reconstruction.
- Three to five scene anchors remain traceable across Panels 2–4.
- Light direction and spatial hierarchy remain coherent.

### Progression

- Panel 2 reads as emotional memory, not a filter.
- Panel 3 reads as structural analysis, not a painting or miniature redraw.
- Panel 4 reads as a genuine synthesis, not a blend effect.
- The sequence changes progressively and does not look like four unrelated styles.

### Restraint

- No generic decorative Japanese symbols appear.
- No fantasy subject, new landmark, extra person, invented text, or ornamental collage element appears.
- Texture supports memory without overpowering the photographic origin.
- Negative space remains intentional.

### Technical finish

- Exactly four equal panels.
- Exact final ratio of 1:2.
- No seams, borders, labels, text, logos, or watermarks.
- No stretched, distorted, or visibly mismatched panels.

## 7. Correction rules

If a panel fails, revise that panel while keeping the successful source map and anchors.

- If the scene becomes unrecognizable: restore two major anchors and the original light opening; reduce abstraction one level.
- If it looks like a filter: rebuild from extracted shapes, material traces, rhythm, and negative space rather than applying a global effect.
- If it looks like an illustration or painting: restore photographic fragments, real material evidence, and source-derived tonal variation.
- If it looks like a generic poster: remove invented symbols, decorative geometry, text-like marks, and unrelated accent colors.
- If the four panels feel unrelated: reapply the same crop, anchor positions, palette family, light invariant, and emotional invariant.
- If Panel 4 is only a blend: reconstruct it from one photographic trace, one structural relationship, and one memory treatment rather than mixing finished panels.
- If Panel 1 changed: discard it and replace it with the untouched source crop during deterministic assembly.
- If typography or a watermark appears: regenerate only the affected interpretation with an explicit no-text constraint, then reassemble.

## 8. Delivery

Return the final quartet as one image. Briefly name the scene anchors and the emotional and light invariants used. Do not place that explanation inside the artwork.
